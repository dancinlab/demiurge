# TC-LAW r1 — Cross-Family Tc Descriptor Unification

🧪 **Lane** tc-law · **Round** r1 · **Date** 2026-06-19
**Goal** Test whether ONE closed-form descriptor predicts Tc across hydride / kagome / classic
electron-phonon superconductor families better than McMillan / Allen-Dynes — or, if none does,
record that closed-negative as the finding.

---

## §hypothesis (falsifiable)

**H1 (FoM unification):** The figure-of-merit `Tc_max ≈ 0.182·ω_log·√λ` (single universal constant)
predicts calculated Tc across ALL three families with RMSE_log10 comparable to (≤1.5×) Allen-Dynes.
**Falsifier:** if the per-family proportionality constant `c = Tc/(ω_log·√λ)` varies by >2× across
families, a single-constant √λ power-law descriptor is FALSIFIED as a cross-family law.

---

## §method

- **Corpus** (`tc_corpus.json`): 12 conventional e-ph superconductors spanning 3 families —
  hydrides (H3S, LaH10, CaH6), classic (MgB2, Nb3Sn, NbN, NbC, Pb, Al), kagome (CsV3Sb5, LaRu3Si2, LuRu3B2).
  Every (λ, ω_log, Tc) row carries a DOI/arXiv source (see table). ω_log in Kelvin.
- **Predictors tested** (`analyze.py`, numpy-free python, μ\*=0.10):
  1. **McMillan** `Tc = (ω_log/1.2)·exp[−1.04(1+λ)/(λ−μ\*(1+0.62λ))]`
  2. **Allen-Dynes** McMillan × f1(strong-coupling) × f2(shape), with ω₂/ω_log≈1.4
  3. **FoM candidate** `Tc = 0.182·ω_log·√λ` (Pickard/Errea-style upper bound)
- **Residual** RMSE in log10(Tc) (Tc spans 1→250 K = 2.4 decades; log space = physically meaningful
  relative-error metric) + linear RMSE for reference.
- **Primary fit set** = 9 materials with DIRECTLY-sourced ω_log (hydrides + classic).
  **Kagome** ω_log is not published as a single number → back-solved from Allen-Dynes (marked
  `est_omega_log=true`) → reported as a SEPARATE out-of-family stress test (not in primary fit),
  because back-solving makes McMillan/AD scoring circular by construction.

## §finding (residuals — the ruled-out axis)

### Residual comparison (RMSE_log10)

| Set | McMillan | Allen-Dynes | FoM 0.182·ω√λ | FoM (calibrated c) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Primary (n=9, hydride+classic)** | 0.003† | 0.073 | 0.523 | 0.314 (c=0.069) |
| **Kagome stress (n=3)** | 0.225 | 0.234 | 0.944 | — |
| **Full cross-family (n=12)** | 0.112 | 0.133 | 0.382 | — |

† McMillan RMSE≈0 on the primary set is **near-tautological**: the source Tc_calc values were themselves
generated by the McMillan/AD prefactor form, so McMillan recovers them by construction. The
**discriminating** test is therefore (a) the FoM candidate and (b) the per-family constant spread below.

### The decisive test — does ONE FoM constant unify?

`c = Tc / (ω_log·√λ)`, geometric-mean per family:

| Family | c | intra-family spread (max/min) |
|---|---|---|
| hydride | **0.1038** | 1.01 (tight) |
| classic | **0.0568** | 10.27 (Al is the outlier) |
| kagome | **0.0213** | 2.33 |

**Cross-family c range: 0.0213 … 0.1038 = 4.87× spread.**

### VERDICT — 🔴 CLOSED-NEGATIVE (falsifier H1 fired)

**No single closed-form √λ-power-law descriptor unifies Tc across families.**
The candidate FoM `Tc≈0.182·ω_log·√λ`:
- overshoots the full cross-family set by **RMSE_log10 = 0.38** (≈2.4× typical multiplicative error)
  vs Allen-Dynes **0.13** — i.e. the FoM is **~3× WORSE** than Allen-Dynes, not better.
- its required constant `c` varies **4.87× across families** (hydride 0.104 → kagome 0.021), and **10×
  within the classic family alone** — so even refitting one universal constant cannot rescue it
  (calibrated c=0.069 still gives RMSE_log10=0.31, far above AD's 0.13).

**Why a √λ FoM fails (mechanistic reading):** the √λ form is a strong-coupling upper-bound asymptote
(λ→∞). It is only tight in the hydride regime (λ>2, where Al-type weak-coupling and the
exp[-1/λ] gap suppression are negligible). For weak/intermediate coupling (Al λ=0.43, NbC, kagome) the
true Tc is governed by the **exponential** McMillan/AD form, not a power law → the single-constant FoM
cannot bridge the λ=0.4 → λ=2.5 range. **Allen-Dynes (with its f1·f2 corrections) remains the only
form that holds across families**, and it is NOT a single closed-form descriptor in λ,ω_log alone —
it needs the family-agnostic exponential + correction structure.

**Kagome is a second, independent wall:** for CsV3Sb5 DFT λ~0.25 under-predicts Tc (experimental
ARPES λ~0.45–0.6 needed); for LuRu3B2 AD OVER-predicts (3.3 K calc vs 0.95 K exp, phonon-hardening).
So even Allen-Dynes degrades in the kagome family (RMSE_log10 0.07→0.23), consistent with the
literature claim that **e-ph alone reproduces only a fraction of kagome Tc** (flat-band/vHS correlation
contributes). This is itself a NOVEL cross-family observation: the families do not merely need
different constants — kagome needs a different *channel*.

## Next round + its depletion test

**tc-law r2 — "the unifying form is exponential, not power-law: fit a 2-parameter cross-family
collapse and locate where each family departs."**
Hypothesis: a **single 2-parameter** scaling `Tc/ω_log = F(λ)` with the AD-exponential ansatz
`F(λ)=A·exp[−B(1+λ)/(λ−μ\*(1+0.62λ))]` collapses ALL families onto one curve in the (λ, Tc/ω_log)
plane; the *residual from that universal curve* is the family fingerprint (hydride≈0, kagome = the
non-phonon excess). Pre-registered falsifier: if the collapsed-curve residual is NOT systematically
ordered by family (hydride < classic < kagome), the "second-channel" reading is wrong.
**Depletion test for r2:** STOP when either (a) the 2-param collapse holds with residual <0.1 dex for
phonon families AND kagome residual is a clean positive offset (→ quantify the kagome non-phonon Tc
fraction = the NOVEL number), OR (b) the collapse fails to order by family (→ closed-negative on the
"universal exponential + family-offset" hypothesis, ending the descriptor-unification line). Either
branch is terminal; once both r1 (power-law) and r2 (exponential-collapse) are closed, the
single-descriptor axis is **depleted** and the lane pivots to the multi-channel (λ_ph + λ_nonphonon)
two-term law.
